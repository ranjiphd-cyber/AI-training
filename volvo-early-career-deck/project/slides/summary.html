Core message: the answer first. We should hire graduates for the company we are becoming, not the one we were. Talking track: If you remember one slide, make it this one. One: our graduate selection model was designed for a mechanical OEM. It is good at confirming exam skills, and it never actually observes the things that predict leadership in a transformation: solving problems with no single right answer, leading peers, commercial sense, and how fast someone learns. Two: we replace it with an immersive assessment lab of four pillars: a hackathon, a business simulation, a group dynamics exercise and a leadership panel, all scored against one persona with six pillars. Three: we put every hire straight onto a flagship transformation project, never into a legacy maintenance role. And the ask is deliberately small: a pilot at three to five campuses next season, measured honestly against a group hired the old way. The rest of the deck is the evidence behind each of these three points. Visual direction: Minto pyramid on one slide; governing thought as the headline, three numbered supporting arguments, and the ask in a navy band.
