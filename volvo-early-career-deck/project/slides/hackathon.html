Core message: the hackathon replaces “tell me about a project” with “show me”. Talking track: Candidates work in mixed teams for 24 to 48 hours on problem briefs written by our own business units. These are not toy puzzles: how do you roll back an over-the-air update safely on a truck in the field, how do you schedule megawatt charging across a depot to avoid peak tariffs, how do you grade a used battery for second life. Our engineers act as mentors, which doubles as a recruitment experience: candidates meet the people they would work with. We score five criteria on a 1-to-5 scale with written anchors, and the bars show the weights. System design and prototype velocity carry half the score, because building is what the role demands. Problem framing asks whether they reframed the brief around a real user. Teamwork under pressure is what mentors observe: who carries load and who unblocks others. And the pitch tests whether they can explain problem, solution, impact and next step in five minutes. A side benefit: our business units get dozens of fresh prototypes on real problems every season.
