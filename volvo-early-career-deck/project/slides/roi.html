Core message: we will prove the return with data, not assert it. Talking track: The one number that is fixed by design is on the left: one hundred percent of pilot hires go onto flagship projects, and every one of them is compared with a peer hired through the legacy process. On the right are the five metrics I propose we report to this team. Months to independent contribution, because a graduate who is productive in months rather than a year is the most direct return. Retention at 24 months, because the most expensive hire is the one who leaves after we have trained them. Quality of hire, the manager’s rating at twelve months. Offer acceptance at partner campuses, which tells us whether we are winning against tech companies. And the share of the cohort in our leadership pipeline by year five. I have left baselines and targets blank on purpose: HR analytics will pull today’s numbers before the pilot starts, and I would rather commit to targets we can defend. Along the bottom: the investment is mainly assessor time, events and a platform; the return is faster productivity, fewer mis-hires and a deeper bench; and the main risks, inconsistent assessors, bias and a heavy candidate experience, have named controls. Visual direction: hero number in a navy panel, KPI table with today versus target, and an investment-return-risk strip underneath.
