Core message: the only reliable way to see peer leadership is to put candidates among peers and watch, with trained observers and a shared behavioural rubric. Talking track: Six to eight candidates, ninety minutes, and nobody is told who leads. Round one is cooperative: a shared goal and open information, which shows who structures the work. Round two introduces scarcity and conflicting incentives between sub-teams; this is where we see conflict resolution and influence without authority, exactly what a graduate needs when they join a cross-functional SDV programme and own nothing but their ideas. Round three rotates roles, and one candidate must brief the panel on the outcome, which tests articulation and honesty about what did not work. Assessors score five behaviours, and the rubric names both the strong signal and the red flag. Notice that the red flags include the loud, dominant candidate: winning by volume or rank is not leadership. We assign one trained assessor per two candidates and run a calibration discussion after every session, which is our main control for bias.
