Core message: the business has already changed direction; our graduate hiring has not. Talking track: Read the left side as a before-and-after. Every row is a shift this team has already committed to: from diesel to electric and fuel-cell, from selling a vehicle once to selling uptime, energy and transport as a service, from component depth to systems, software, data and AI, from multi-year programmes to continuous over-the-air releases, and from fleet buyer to partner in a logistics ecosystem. Our public ambition is a net-zero value chain by 2040. Each of these shifts changes the job of a young engineer. Three consequences for talent on the right. Skills date fast, so learning speed matters more than what someone memorised for an exam. The talent war has moved: we now compete with technology companies and EV start-ups, who sell graduates speed, ownership and impact. And errors compound: the cohort we hire next season is the bench we will promote in the 2030s. If we select for the old company, we will be led by the old company.
