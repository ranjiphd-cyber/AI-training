Core message: the business has already changed direction; our graduate hiring has not. Talking track: Look at the left side. Every row is a shift this team has already committed to: from diesel to battery-electric and fuel-cell, from selling a vehicle once to selling uptime, energy and transport as a service, from multi-year model programmes to continuous over-the-air releases. Our public ambition is a net-zero value chain by 2040. Each of these shifts changes the job of a young engineer. They no longer optimise one component; they work across software, hardware, energy and service, and they make commercial calls early in their careers. Three consequences on the right. One: technical skills date quickly, so learning velocity matters more than what someone memorised for an exam. Two: we now compete for the same IIT and NIT graduates as technology companies and EV start-ups, who sell them speed, ownership and impact. Three: hiring mistakes compound. The cohort we hire next season is the bench we will promote into leadership in the 2030s. If we select for the old company, we will be led by the old company.
